# Result figures — campaign_T1 (legacy-study)

> **Historical results.** Every figure here is built from the approved Stage 14 official campaign (490 runs, seeds 1-10), which was produced with the **legacy-study** vehicle policy (first queued car at green + 3.9 s, permissive first 2.0 s of yellow). The project's current standard policy (Stop on Yellow, first car at green onset) was adopted afterwards; these figures are **not** standard-policy results and must not be presented as such.

Generated by `python -m smarttraffic.make_graphs` (Stage 15) from the committed CSV summaries; each CSV is verified against the SHA-256 in its manifest. Re-running gives the same `figures_values.csv` and `figures_manifest.json`.

* Campaign folder: `results/official/campaign_T1` · Smart-T1 lock `3b3da0427f7f965f23a1c586a782fbeb3be8445608dc2eaf4135f5512e3ca84c`
* Statistics: mean over 10 seeds, 95 % t-interval; paired differences use the same seeds; no multiple-comparison adjustment (as the study specifies).
* Formats: `.png` (300 dpi) for slides, `.pdf` / `.svg` (vector) for the report.
* Controllers: FT-A (study fixed plan), FT-B (per-scenario Webster plan; equal to FT-A in S1, S3, S5), Smart-T1 (locked τ 15 s, W_MAX 90 s), FT-TOD (two-plan time-of-day, S6 only), Smart-study (historical comparison only), Smart-T1-noWMAX (S10 extra only).
* `figures_values.csv`: every plotted value (table view of all figures).

## Interpretation

### F01_mean_delay_by_scenario — Mean control delay by scenario
* Lowest mean delay per scenario: S1 FT-A = FT-B, S2 FT-B, S3 Smart-T1, S4 Smart-T1, S5 FT-A = FT-B, S6 FT-TOD, S7a FT-B, S7b Smart-T1, S8 FT-B, S9 FT-B, S10 FT-B.
* S4 (very low demand): Smart-T1 15.6 s vs FT-A 30.8 s and FT-B 24.8 s.
* S6: Smart-T1 48.8 s vs FT-A 175.5 s, FT-B 240.1 s; the two-plan FT-TOD reaches 43.8 s.
* S1 and S5: Smart-T1 is higher than FT-A/FT-B (56.8 vs 43.5 s; 59.5 vs 49.7 s).

### F02_p95_delay — 95th-percentile control delay
* S6 P95: Smart-T1 112 s vs FT-A 416 s, FT-B 538 s, FT-TOD 139 s.

### F03_max_delay — Maximum control delay
* S8 FT-A max 2676 s (left-turn demand exceeds FT-A's 8 s left greens) vs Smart-T1 329 s.

### F04_throughput_unfinished — Throughput and unfinished vehicles
* S7b (oversaturated): throughput FT-A 2638, FT-B 2802, Smart-T1 2905 veh/h; unfinished FT-A 510, FT-B 291, Smart-T1 164 vehicles per run.
* Scenarios with unfinished vehicles (mean > 0.5 per run): FT-A: S2, S7b, S8, S9; FT-B: S7a, S7b, S10; Smart-T1: S7b.

### F05_mean_queue — Average queue length
* S8 mean queue FT-A 53.4 veh vs Smart-T1 5.7 veh per movement.

### F06_jain_fairness — Fairness between movements (Jain's index)
* Lowest fairness: S9 FT-A 0.30.
* Smart-T1 mean index higher than FT-B in S1, S3, S4, S5, S6, S7a, S8, S10; lower in S2, S7b, S9 (point estimates; see the CIs).

### F07a_long_waits — Vehicles delayed more than 90 s
* S1: Smart-T1 404 vs FT-A 129 vehicles over 90 s per run (more long waits with Smart-T1 at moderate balanced demand).

### F07b_S10_starvation_WMAX — S10: starvation hold (W_MAX) is a threshold, not a hard bound
* Smart-T1 longest call wait in S10: mean 156 s (max 166 s): above 90 s in every seed, so W_MAX = 90 s is a hold threshold, not a hard bound.
* Without W_MAX the longest wait rises to 219 s and mean delay to 314 s (Smart-T1 174 s).

### F08a_signal_changes — Signal changes per hour
* FT-A 308-308, FT-B 230-442, Smart-T1 200-240 green onsets per hour across scenarios.

### F08b_green_utilization — Green utilization
* Smart-T1 utilization below both fixed plans in S1, S2, S3, S5, S6, S7a, S7b, S9, S10; above both in S4. Lower utilization means more of the displayed green passes without a saturated discharge.

### F09_SmartT1_vs_FTA — Smart-T1 vs FT-A: difference in mean control delay
* Smart-T1 higher than FT-A: S1, S5.
* Smart-T1 lower than FT-A: S2, S3, S4, S6, S7a, S7b, S8, S9, S10.

### F10_SmartT1_vs_FTB — Smart-T1 vs FT-B: difference in mean control delay
* Smart-T1 higher than FT-B: S1, S2, S5, S9, S10.
* Smart-T1 lower than FT-B: S3, S4, S6.
* No significant difference from FT-B: S7a, S7b, S8.

### F11_SmartT1_vs_SmartStudy_historical — Historical: Smart-T1 vs Smart-study (tuning vs final evaluation)
* Tuning (S1, seeds 101-105): Smart-T1 54.97 s vs Smart-study 58.55 s.
* Evaluation (seeds 1-10): S1 -1.2 s, S2 -12.6 s, S3 -1.1 s, S4 -0.0 s, S5 -3.2 s, S6 -5.8 s, S7a -2.8 s, S7b -50.6 s, S8 -0.9 s, S9 +10.2 s, S10 -20.3 s; the improvement is not robust (CI includes 0 in most scenarios, S9 worse).

### F12_S6_time_varying_demand — S6: demand shifts sides at t = 1800 s
* Mean total queue before / after the shift (veh): FT-A 85 / 87; FT-B 110 / 121; Smart-T1 25 / 26; FT-TOD 23 / 23.
* FT-A and FT-B keep one plan for the whole hour; with N-S heavy (first half) and E-W heavy (second half) the heavy axis exceeds that plan's capacity, so their queues grow in each half and only fall while the previously heavy side clears after the shift. Smart-T1 re-allocates green from detector calls and FT-TOD switches to a second plan at 1800 s; both keep the total queue roughly flat.

### F13_key_scenarios_mean_delay — Key presentation scenarios: mean control delay
* S4: FT-A 31 / FT-B 25 / Smart-T1 16, S6: FT-A 176 / FT-B 240 / Smart-T1 49 / FT-TOD 44, S7b: FT-A 762 / FT-B 642 / Smart-T1 612, S8: FT-A 635 / FT-B 69 / Smart-T1 72, S10: FT-A 231 / FT-B 130 / Smart-T1 174 s/veh.

## Notes

* W_MAX = 90 s is a starvation-hold threshold, not a proven upper bound on waiting: detector-call waits exceed 90 s (F07b), yet removing W_MAX makes S10 substantially worse.
* The Smart-T1 parameters were selected on S1 seeds 101-105; F11 shows that the gain over Smart-study did not carry over robustly to the evaluation seeds.
* Figures use no truncated axes: every bar axis starts at zero; small multiples state that each panel has its own scale.
